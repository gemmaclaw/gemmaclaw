Gemma 4 on a 24 GB GPU

Gemma 4 launched on April 2, 2026 in two variants: a 26B A4B MoE model with about 3.8B active parameters per token, and a 31B dense model. Both target consumer-class GPUs, but only one is actually a good fit on a single 24 GB card. We ran the Gemmaclaw benchmark harness against both, plus the Gemma 3 4B baseline, on an RTX 3090.

The 26B MoE model clears 98% on quality at interactive speed. The 31B dense model is impractical on consumer hardware. Numbers below.

Benchmark snapshot

Hardware: NVIDIA RTX 3090 (24 GB), AMD Ryzen 9 5900X, 30 GB system RAM, WSL2 Ubuntu. All models Q4_K_M. Quality is scored by an LLM judge across 15 tool-use-style tasks (instruction following, reasoning, extraction, safety, coding). Throughput measured at the local HTTP API.

Model Backend VRAM Score Pass rate Median tok/s
Gemma 3 4B Ollama ~3 GB 134/140 (96%) 100% (15/15) 178
Gemma 4 26B MoE Ollama ~17 GB 137/140 (98%) 100% (15/15) 117
Gemma 4 26B MoE llama.cpp standalone ~17 GB 127/140 (91%) 93.3% (14/15) 133
Gemma 4 31B dense Ollama ~21 GB 97/140 (69%) 73% (11/15) 2.6
Reproduce: gemmaclaw benchmark --model gemma4:26b  ·  Full per-task breakdown, methodology, and HTML dashboards in benchmark-results/BENCHMARK-SUMMARY.md.

What this means in practice

  • Gemma 4 26B MoE is the sweet spot for a 24 GB GPU. 98% quality, about 117 tok/s on Ollama, comfortable VRAM headroom for KV cache. The MoE design activates only a roughly 4B-parameter subset per token, which is why a 26B model fits and runs this fast on consumer hardware.
  • llama.cpp standalone is meaningfully faster for the same model. About 14% higher median throughput and roughly half the p50 latency, because it skips the request orchestration layer. The single missed task was a thinking-template artifact, not a quality regression. Both backends are now blessed for Gemma 4: pick llama.cpp for raw speed, Ollama for zero-config plus rendering-aware output parsing for thinking models.
  • Gemma 3 4B is still useful when speed wins. Roughly 50% faster than 26B MoE with only a 2-point quality gap. Good fit for latency-sensitive or batch workloads.
  • Skip Gemma 4 31B dense on a 24 GB card. Q4_K_M loads at about 21 GB, leaving very little room for KV cache. CPU offload pressure drops throughput to about 2.6 tok/s, and coding tasks time out before they can finish. The 31B dense variant needs 40+ GB VRAM (A6000, L40, dual-GPU) to be practical.
